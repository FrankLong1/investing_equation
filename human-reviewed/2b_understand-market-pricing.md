## Purpose

For each debate identified in [2A](./2a_identify-key-debates.md), work through
what the market appears to believe, what assumptions underpin that view, and
how much of it is reflected in forecasts, valuation, and price.

## 1. Gather Expectations

For each key debate, identify the specific numbers it turns on—for example,
next-quarter revenue, next-year EPS, or a segment's operating margin. Gather
expectations for those numbers and the qualitative views behind them from
the sources below, then cross-check against positioning. Include informal
expectations from meeting transcripts, commentary, and discussions; identify
whether each is a speaker's own forecast, a secondhand report, or a whisper.

- **Management Guidance:** What metric, period, and number or range has management guided to, and under what operating assumptions?

- **Buy-Side**

  Use direct conversations, especially buy-side dinners and round-the-room
  questions, to establish:

  - **Views and Forecasts:** What do investors believe about the key debates, and what numbers are they modeling, including for individual segments?
  - **Bogeys:** What results would count as a beat or disappointment, and how do those hurdles differ from their forecasts?
  - **Positioning:** What do they own, short, or avoid, at what size, and why? Include relevant theme allocations and specify gross, net, or another exposure measure.
  - **Potential Changes:** What evidence or price would change their view or position?

  Record who said it and when. Keep beliefs, reported positioning, intended
  actions, and our interpretation separate; one room does not establish
  market consensus. Carry positioning observations into Step 0's
  investor-base analysis.

- **Sell-Side Consensus**

  - **Consensus:** What is the published estimate, how wide is the range, and when were the underlying forecasts updated?
  - **Segment Models:** What revenue, margin, and profit assumptions sit beneath the headline number in individual analysts' models?

- **Consensus-Setting Checks and Metrics**

  - **Shared Reference Points:** Which channel checks or third-party datasets shape investor expectations, and whose? Identify the underlying population, measurement, and access rights.
  - **Implied Expectations:** What does the latest reading measure, and how do investors translate it into forecasts or beat/miss hurdles?

  Source influence and measurement accuracy are separate; repeated references
  to the same check are not independent evidence.

- **Positioning Cross-Check:** Do observed holdings, exposures, and flows support stated beliefs? Allow for hedges, mandates, disclosure lags, and price-driven weight changes. Disclosed long holdings alone do not establish net exposure. See the positioning source guide in Appendix C.

## 2. Work Backward From Price

- **Required Performance:** What revenue growth, margins, and duration of success would justify the current price? Reverse engineer a growth/margin assumption from the multiple, making reinvestment, terminal value, and required return explicit.
- **Debate Sensitivity:** What range for the debated variable is consistent with price, conditional on the other assumptions? Work through individual segments and their contributions to value where relevant.

## 3. Compare Related Companies

- **Identify Comps:** Which companies or individual business segments are comparable?
- **Build Comp Tables:** How do valuation multiples, expected growth, and margins compare on a consistent basis? Do this at the segment level where relevant.
- **Explain the Gap:** If a valuation gap exists, what differences in expectations, business quality, capital intensity, or risk could explain it?

## Differences Across Investor Cohorts

Apply this lens across the three methods above: different groups may focus
on different drivers, use different assumptions, or understand different
parts of the business.

- **Relevant Cohorts:** Which analyst and investor groups follow the stock, and which are missing from our evidence?
- **Different Beliefs:** Where do their forecasts or valuation assumptions differ, and why? Support differences with specific comments or models rather than group labels.
- **Pricing Influence:** Which views appear to influence current pricing, and what evidence supports that assessment? Coverage, ownership, and willingness to trade are different things.

**Illustrative hypothesis — diversified financial firm:** Analysts focused on
existing fee businesses may place less weight on demand from a new technology
cycle than investors following its supply chain. Test whether that difference
actually appears in their assumptions, then trace the new demand through
financing opportunities, capital deployed, fee-paying assets, fee rates, and
profitability. A difference in attention only becomes a useful expectations gap
if it changes the financial outlook or valuation; neither cohort should be
presumed correct.

## Output and Handoff to 2C

For each debate, pass to [2C](./2c_form-a-variant-view.md):

- **Market View:** The inferred qualitative view and key metric expectations, with periods and values or ranges.
- **Underlying Assumptions:** The operating and financial assumptions supporting that view.
- **Evidence and Uncertainty:** Supporting sources, meaningful cohort differences, plausible alternatives, and unresolved gaps. Distinguish stated forecasts from assumptions inferred from valuation.

Keep uncertainty about what the market believes separate from the fund's
confidence in its own view.

## Appendix

### A. Study Reactions to New Information

- **Earnings and Guidance**

  - **Surprises:** How did results or guidance differ from the expectations recorded before the release?
  - **Revisions:** Which forecasts and valuation assumptions changed afterward?

- **Company and Industry News**

  - **Relevant Events:** How did the stock respond to product news, competitor results, or industry disclosures affecting the debate?
  - **Confounding News:** What simultaneous information or broader market moves could explain the response?

- **What Surprised the Market?**

  - **Interpretation:** Which prior expectations are consistent with the observed reaction and subsequent commentary?
  - **Limits:** Could a muted response reflect anticipation, offsetting news, low materiality, or delayed incorporation?
  - **Price Setters:** Which investors appear to have changed their views or trading behavior, and what evidence supports that inference?

### B. Options and Other Securities

- **Options-Implied Outcomes and Uncertainty**

  - **Priced Outcomes:** What terminal-price ranges, downside risks, and event uncertainty are reflected in options?
  - **Interpretation:** What assumptions would be needed to connect those price outcomes to the business debate?

  Options imply risk-adjusted pricing distributions, not a direct measurement
  of investors' real-world probabilities for business outcomes.

- **Signals From Bonds and Other Instruments**

  - **Related Claims:** What do bonds or other relevant instruments imply about financing, solvency, or the same underlying exposure?
  - **Consistency:** Do those signals support or challenge the equity interpretation, after allowing for different payoffs and risk premia?

### C. Positioning Source Guide

- **Holdings:** Use 13F, [N-PORT](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-n-port-data-sets), and fund disclosures to identify owners, allocations, and changes. Check disclosure dates and portfolio coverage.
- **Prime Brokers and Custodians:** Use available aggregate exposure, crowding, and flow data; record which client universe is represented.
- **Shorts and Borrow:** Track [reported short interest](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/short-interest), shares on loan, utilization, and borrow fees. Short-sale volume is not outstanding short interest.
- **Fund and Retail Flows:** Use appropriately licensed fund-flow, allocation, and brokerage-activity data to track participation and changes. Record the source population and reporting lag.
- **Research Providers:** Evaluate specialist research and sell-side flow reports for coverage, methodology, incentives, and timeliness.
- **Prime-Broker Reports:** Where lawfully available, use aggregate client-flow data with its coverage and confidentiality limits documented.
