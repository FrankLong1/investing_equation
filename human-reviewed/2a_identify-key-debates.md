## Purpose

Use the stock-driver map and retained external context to identify the key
unresolved questions that matter to the stock. Define the competing answers
before estimating which answers the market is pricing in [2B](./2b_understand-market-pricing.md).

## Types of Debates

| Type | Core Question |
| --- | --- |
| **Demand and Growth** | How large can demand become, and how quickly? |
| **Competition and Share** | Who wins customers, revenue, and pricing power? |
| **Business Economics** | Does growth translate into durable margins, cash flow, and attractive investment returns? |
| **Structural Change** | Could a new technology or business model fundamentally expand or impair the business? |
| **Valuation** | What multiple or valuation framework is justified by the business's prospects and risks? |
| **Event and Policy Outcomes** | Will a consequential event occur, or a policy change take effect, and with what economic consequences? |
| **Positioning and Flows** | Could crowding, leverage, or changes in ownership materially affect price? |

Use these as prompts for finding debates. A debate may span several types;
write the specific unresolved question rather than stopping at the category.

## Key Questions

- **Current Debates:** What questions recur in sell-side research, management Q&A, and meeting transcripts?
- **Underlying Uncertainty:** What exactly is unresolved about the driver: magnitude, timing, durability, or mechanism?
- **Competing Answers:** What are the plausible answers, and what evidence or reasoning supports each?
- **Materiality:** Would different answers materially change the stock's earnings, valuation, or trading dynamics?

A debate should be a specific question. For example: “Are current margins
sustainable as capacity expands?” is a debate; “margins” is only a topic.

## What Aren't We Asking?

- **Overlooked Debates:** What important uncertainty is missing from current discussion, including around emerging drivers?
- **Unchallenged Assumptions:** What are we treating as settled that could materially change the outlook if wrong?
- **Missing Connections:** What changes in customers, suppliers, competitors, or adjacent industries have we not connected to this stock?

Turn material questions into entries in the debate map, even if no one else
is discussing them. Keep the evidence that a question matters separate from
the claim that the market has overlooked it; assess market awareness in 2B.

## From a Debate to Price

$$

where $b$ is a key debate, $d$ is the material driver it affects, and
$\mathbf{x}$ is the set of Step 1A price inputs through which it reaches price.

## Output: Key Debate Map

For each debate, record the question, affected driver, competing answers,
supporting sources, materiality rationale, and relevant horizon. Carry open
questions into [2B](./2b_understand-market-pricing.md); identifying a debate
does not require a variant view.

### Example: Hypothetical Search and Cloud Platform Debate Map

This is an **illustrative candidate map** for a fictional platform company, not
a claim about any actual issuer or current market debate. The questions and
perspectives demonstrate how to structure research before estimating what is
priced in.

“AI Search” is a topic. “Will AI answers expand or erode search economics?” is
a debate.

| Key Debate | Viable Perspectives | Why It Matters to the Stock | Evidence That Would Distinguish the Perspectives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Search Usage:** Will users keep bringing valuable queries to the platform as AI assistants improve? | • The platform retains the habit and gains new usage.<br>• It retains simple queries but loses valuable research and shopping journeys.<br>• Displacement remains limited to certain categories. | Search volume and the commercial value of the query mix. | Usage by query category, shopping journeys, referral patterns, and user retention. |
| **Search Monetization:** Can AI answers generate as much advertising revenue per commercial interaction as traditional search? | • Better intent understanding improves monetization.<br>• Fewer ad opportunities reduce monetization.<br>• Performance varies substantially by query type. | Revenue generated from retained traffic. | Ad engagement, advertiser conversion, revenue per comparable interaction, and adoption of AI ad formats. |
| **Search Costs:** Can efficiency gains offset the additional cost of answering queries with AI? | • Lower inference costs and better routing preserve margins.<br>• Richer answers consume the savings.<br>• Margins weaken during a transition and then recover. | Profit earned on Search revenue. | Serving-cost trends, model routing, compute intensity, and margin progression. |
| **Cloud Growth:** Does AI demand create durable, broad customer spending? | • AI workloads become recurring production usage.<br>• Spending stays concentrated in a few large customers.<br>• Experimentation grows faster than durable deployment. | Cloud's sustainable revenue growth. | Production deployments, renewals, customer concentration, backlog conversion, and expansion within customers. |
| **Cloud Economics:** Does AI infrastructure growth translate into attractive profits? | • Utilization and differentiated technology support returns.<br>• Competition passes most savings to customers.<br>• Revenue grows but remains capital intensive. | Cloud profit contribution and the value of that growth. | Pricing, utilization, depreciation, operating margins, and capital required per dollar of revenue. |
| **Investment Returns:** Will the infrastructure build generate enough incremental cash to justify its cost? | • Demand absorbs capacity at attractive returns.<br>• Capacity arrives ahead of demand.<br>• Ongoing replacement spending keeps cash generation constrained. | Free cash flow and returns on capital. | Capacity utilization, incremental operating cash flow, capital expenditure, and equipment replacement needs. |
